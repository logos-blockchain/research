Profit per claim, across the token price

The crossing is the break-even token price: below it a claim costs more electricity than it earns, and a rational miner stops. It moves in proportion to both the electricity price and the difficulty, so a chain whose difficulty has risen tenfold needs a tenfold higher token price to keep the same miners.

Under four simulated price histories

Left: four stylised token-price paths, in multiples of a long-run reference price you set below. They are not backtests — they are hand-built from the qualitative behaviour each asset is known for, and exist to show which conclusions survive which kind of history. Right: the profit per claim each path implies, with the break-even line. Time below it is time when mining does not pay.
