Total Beer Inference

One die each, one bottle cap each, and a circle. Nobody announces anything and yet the table gives itself away.

Setup

  • Four to six players in a circle. One die each, one bottle cap each, beer.
  • Everyone rolls once, in secret, for their limit. Roll again if you get a six — a six never drinks, and the circle would jam solid.

A round — five seconds

  1. Got a cap? Roll. No cap, sit it out and wait for one to reach you.
  2. Rolled above your limit? Take a sip, and pass one cap to your right.
Roll high, drink, hand it on.

When the beer is gone

Everyone names who they think has the lowest limit — the one who has been trying hardest to drink all night. Wrong guess, you buy.

Why that is harder than it sounds

Everybody drinks exactly the same amount. A limit of one is five times more eager than a limit of five, and it makes no difference at all — the cap has to come round the circle before you can roll again, so the whole table moves at the pace of its slowest member.

Counting sips tells you nothing. What gives someone away is who is never holding a cap. Four thousand rounds, limits one through five:

The other game — one rule different

The circle above is about bottlenecks. If you want the one the talk is actually about — a count that comes out short — stop using caps as permission to roll and use them as a record of what got missed:

  1. Nobody passes anything. Everyone rolls every round.
  2. Six — sip, and shout BEER.  One — sip, and say nothing.
  3. Quiet sip and nobody else shouted that round? Drop a cap in the middle.
  4. A Barman sits with their back turned and ticks a beer mat whenever they hear a shout. Two shouts is still one tick.

After twelve rounds: ticks are what the Barman knows, ticks plus caps are what happened. With five at the table the mat comes out at 0.69 of the truth.

Now allow vouching — when you shout you may add “…and one went quiet, two back”, and if they own up a cap comes off the pile. Reach back three rounds, one rescue per shout. The pile empties.

Why they are rigged. Both are Cryptarchia, played with dice. In the second, a roll is the block lottery, a sip is a block, a round is a slot, the mat is the chain, a cap is an orphan and a vouch is an uncle reference — the simulator puts the count at 0.64 to 0.74 of the truth and this table lands at 0.65 to 0.73. In the first, the cap is a credit and the circle is a pipeline: it runs at the speed of its slowest hop, which is the same reason the network has to stay under one block in the air. Different lesson, same table.
